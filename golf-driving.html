How to read these pages

Three records: GHIN supplies posted scores and the official Handicap Index; Arccos supplies sensor shots for a local scratch comparison; TrackMan supplies practice measurements.

Strokes gained (SG): +1 means one stroke better than the scratch model; −1 means one worse. Summary rates are per 18 holes unless labeled per shot or actual round. Scratch means a 0.0 handicap.

Median: the middle result. Carry: flight to landing. Total distance: includes roll. Dispersion: how widely shots scatter. Expand the explanations beside a chart for its exact definition.

Evidence: samples are labeled, small groups are flagged, and uncertainty ranges do not account for every model assumption. A daily rebuild does not mean the underlying rounds are recent.

The initial read

The first session points toward more ball speed and steadier contact. My upward strike was already around 5°. Launch and spin were promising in the middle of the sample, but varied between shots.

Initial read: 10 drives at the end of an 81-shot session. I reported feeling tired. This is a starting point, not an established trend.

  1. Find the middle more often

    Use impact-location feedback to tighten the strike pattern. Keep the ordinary shots in the sample, not just the good ones.

  2. Build usable speed

    Explore more club speed while preserving strike quality and direction. The ball-speed range below is an estimate to test, not a promise of 315 yards.

  3. Keep launch and spin together

    My fastest ball in the first session was not my longest carry. Lower spin alone is not the goal; the combination has to keep the ball in the air.

Training for more speed

My first session put club speed at 116.1 mph. The provisional 121–123 mph range means finding roughly 5–7 mph while keeping the strike. Strength and power training can support that goal, but no exercise or six-week block guarantees 315 yards.

Proposed starting plan with gym access. TrackMan cannot identify which muscles are limiting my swing; exercise selection and loading need to reflect training experience. The schedule below is a practical trial, not a tested prescription for my body.

A six-week trial

Monday and Thursday: warm up, short driver-speed block, power work, then strength. Leave at least 48 hours between hard sessions. Replace overlapping gym or speed work with these sessions rather than adding everything on top. Retest on a separate fresh day after six weeks.

Warm up first

Start with 5 minutes of brisk walking or cycling, then 6–8 comfortable bodyweight squats, hip hinges and upper-back rotations per side. Before fast swings, build gradually from half-speed to normal driver speed over 8–10 swings.

Driver-speed practice

After warming up and before lifting, hit 3 sets of 3 drivers with fast intent and a balanced finish. Rest 20–30 seconds between swings and 2 minutes between sets. Use the normal driver and a safe hitting bay; no weighted club is required. If speed or contact keeps falling after a rest, end the block. These nine swings replace extra speed work that day.

Gym exercises · power first, then strength
  1. Vertical jump · 2 sets × 3 jumps

    Practice producing force quickly through the legs. Use bodyweight, reset between jumps and land softly with control. Rest 60–90 seconds between sets; stop when jump height or landing quality drops.

  2. Rotational medicine-ball throw · 2 sets × 3 throws per side

    Practice accelerating through the hips and trunk. Start with a light 2 kg ball and a designated throwing wall or area. Turn through the hips and feet, release fast, then reset. Train both directions; rest 60–90 seconds between sets.

  3. Goblet squat · 2 sets × 6–8 reps

    Build leg strength to support force production. Hold a dumbbell at the chest; sit between the hips and stand through the whole foot. Choose a load that leaves 2–3 good repetitions in reserve. Rest 2 minutes.

  4. Dumbbell Romanian deadlift · 2 sets × 6–8 reps

    Strengthen the hip hinge and the back of the legs. Keep a slight knee bend, send the hips back and keep the weights close. Stop the descent before the back rounds. Leave 2–3 repetitions in reserve; rest 2 minutes.

  5. Split squat · 2 sets × 6 reps per side

    Develop strength and control on each leg. Use a comfortable staggered stance and lower straight down. Start with bodyweight or light dumbbells; hold a support if balance limits the movement. Rest 90 seconds between sets.

  6. Cable row + dumbbell bench press · 2 sets × 8 reps of each

    Build upper-body pulling and pushing strength. Use controlled reps and a comfortable range. Keep 2–3 repetitions in reserve; rest 90–120 seconds between sets. An elevated push-up can replace the press.

  7. Pallof press · 2 sets × 8 reps per side

    Practice trunk control against a sideways pull. Stand side-on to a cable, press the handle away from the chest and resist turning. Pause briefly with arms extended. Use a light load; rest 60 seconds.

How to progress

Weeks 1–2: learn the movements and keep the listed volume. If technique and recovery stay good, weeks 3–6 can add a third set to squats and hinges. Increase a lift by the smallest available weight only when every set reaches the top of its rep range with 2–3 reps still available. Keep jumps and throws brief and fast. Have a qualified trainer check unfamiliar lifts; stop for pain rather than working through it.

Check that it transfers to carry

Log training loads, recovery and driver speed. For the six-week comparison, repeat the fresh 20-drive test with the same ball and simulator settings. Compare median club speed, ball speed, carry and corridor percentage, including ordinary misses. Speed-session results stay in the history but are not directly equivalent to the 20-shot test; this page does not yet tag session type automatically. If speed improves without carry, reassess contact, launch and spin before adding more gym work.

Why ball speed matters
Ball speed is how fast the ball leaves the face. Both swing speed and strike efficiency contribute. TrackMan’s published carry optimizer gives about 310 yards at 120 mph club speed, 179 mph ball speed and a +5° attack angle. That is a reference point, not a personalized prediction.
Why lower spin is not always better
Carry depends on ball speed, launch and spin together. A low-spinning shot can roll a long way without carrying farther. This goal measures where the ball lands, not its final distance.
How reliable is this?
One session establishes a baseline. It cannot establish improvement or prove a swing diagnosis. Sessions with fewer than eight driver shots are marked as small samples and do not set the headline numbers. The export does not establish altitude, temperature or normalization settings; keep those consistent when comparing sessions.
