Archibald, the system that runs my admin

In April 2026 I started building an AI agent system to run my own finances, email and schedule. I had no engineering background.

Personal project · Builder · April 2026 to present

I built it for two reasons. My own admin, from bills and statements to email and scheduling, was taking hours every week. And I wanted to understand AI agents by building one, not by reading about them. My own life was the test case.

Archibald runs only my own accounts. Otian AI grew out of it: Archie, the product we sell, takes what I learned building Archibald and puts it to work for small businesses.

What it does

It works on a schedule, mostly without me. It pulls transactions from six bank and card feeds, reconciles them, checks statements and closes each month. It also sorts my email and keeps my calendar. That gives me back about five hours a week.

It runs on OpenClaw, on a Mac mini. I built it in Claude Code and Codex, in Python. Its skills have automated tests that catch regressions, and guardrails limit what it can do without asking me first.

When “ok” wasn’t true

In October a morning job stopped running for two days. The nightly job that keeps it on the schedule reported “ok” three nights in a row.

The check confirmed that the scheduling command had worked. It never confirmed that the job would run. The job had been switched off by hand, and the command will update a switched-off job without complaint.

I changed the check to confirm the job will actually run, and added an alert after the first failure. Twelve of the 145 live jobs now exist only to audit the system’s own accuracy.

It’s the same lesson as the $1.8M power bill. A report that says everything matches isn’t proof. Check the thing itself.

The chart counts every job ever added to Archibald’s schedule, including ones later switched off, from its own job database. The run count adds up each job’s run counter. The log keeps only each job’s latest 2,000 runs, so the 99% success rate is measured on those.
