My game, explained

How the measurements fit together, what they suggest, and what I am testing next.

GHIN checked 2026-10-29. On-course analysis: 5 rounds / 81 holes, 2026-04-15 to 2026-06-04.

How to read these pages

Three records: GHIN supplies posted scores and the official Handicap Index; Arccos supplies sensor shots for a local scratch comparison; TrackMan supplies practice measurements.

Strokes gained (SG): +1 means one stroke better than the scratch model; −1 means one worse. Summary rates are per 18 holes unless labeled per shot or actual round. Scratch means a 0.0 handicap.

Median: the middle result. Carry: flight to landing. Total distance: includes roll. Dispersion: how widely shots scatter. Expand the explanations beside a chart for its exact definition.

Evidence: samples are labeled, small groups are flagged, and uncertainty ranges do not account for every model assumption. A daily rebuild does not mean the underlying rounds are recent.

Where I stand

4.1Handicap Index

My index is 4.1, my recent 18-hole GHIN adjusted scores average 81.8, and the complete 18-hole rounds in my sensor sample averaged 85.8 raw strokes. Separately, the model estimates 10 strokes lost per 18 holes across all 81 sensor-tracked holes, including the nine-hole round.

Those three numbers disagree on purpose, and that is the first thing worth understanding about my own data.

Handicap Index
What a Handicap Index is. A measure of scoring potential, not average score. With at least 20 scores, it generally averages the best 8 Score Differentials from the latest 20, with safeguards and adjustments. A differential accounts for course difficulty. Fewer-score records use a different calculation. USGA method.

Hole by hole, those rounds were 4 birdies and 1 eagle, 29 pars, 30 bogeys and 17 doubles or worse across 81 holes. The doubles are 21% of my holes and they are where the round actually goes. Nothing on this page matters as much as how those holes start and end.

Expected strokes
Expected strokes. The model estimates how many shots a scratch player needs to finish from a given position. A hypothetical position worth 3.0 expected strokes followed by one worth 1.8 produces 3.0 − 1.8 − 1 = +0.2 strokes gained.
Strokes gained
Strokes gained. Expected strokes before the shot, minus expected strokes afterward, minus the shot and any penalties. Positive is better than the scratch model; negative is worse. −2.0 per 18 holes means about two strokes lost. Category totals include all shots; the putting distance table covers first putts only.
Why they add up
Why the four categories add up to the total. Every shot belongs to exactly one category, decided by where it was played from: off the tee on a par 4 or 5 is driving, a full shot to the green is approach, anything inside 50 yards that is not a putt is short game, and a putt is putting. Add the four up and you get the whole gap to scratch, which is why they always sum to the total.

The handicap is a separate chain that runs alongside it, built from scores rather than shots. That is why rounds I played without the sensors still move my index but never appear on the strokes gained pages.

Differential
Score Differential. A score adjusted for course difficulty and playing conditions. Lower is better. The colors here compare past differentials with my current index, not necessarily the index I held on that date.
Scratch
What scratch means. Scratch means a 0.0 Handicap Index. Strokes gained here uses a locally calibrated scratch model. PGA Tour comparisons are separate and explicitly labeled; a scratch golfer is not a tour professional.

The four parts, and how they feed each other

Four parts, in the order the shots happen. Read the estimates alongside their ranges; even a range that excludes zero depends on the model and this small sample.

The ranges
How to read the uncertainty ranges. These approximate 95% intervals describe sampling uncertainty under the model assumptions. They are not a 95% probability that a fixed true value lies inside the displayed band. A range touching or crossing zero does not clearly distinguish the result from scratch. Just five rounds, correlated shots and the calibrated baseline limit certainty beyond what these ranges capture.

What the penalty estimate means. The model compares my penalty tee shots with my clean tee shots across this sample. They are not matched shots on identical holes, so course difficulty and club choice can affect the estimate. It is an association worth investigating, not a guaranteed saving.

Greens in regulation
Green in regulation. On the green in two shots fewer than par: one shot on a par 3, two on a par 4, three on a par 5. Miss it and I need an up and down to save par.
Proximity
Proximity. The median distance from the ball to the hole after an approach: half the measured results finish closer, half farther away. The all-approach sample also includes shots that missed the green. This affects the next shot, but does not by itself diagnose putting skill.
Dispersion
Dispersion and miss tendency. Dispersion describes how widely shots scatter. On the course, lateral and depth figures are sample standard deviations in yards, not a guaranteed percentage within a target. The tendency uses average left/right and long/short offsets. The TrackMan page uses a different spread measure: half the middle 80% range.

Short game

−4.2

−6.2 to −2.1 a round range excludes zero

Missed greens create recovery work, but not every miss leaves a short chip or a chance to save par. This sample separates the recorded greenside chances from longer pitches. I convert 29% of the ones inside 30 yards, 9 of 31. From 30 to 50 yards I am 0 of 16.

Up and down
Up and down. Here: finish the hole in two strokes or fewer, including penalties, from the first non-putting shot inside 30 yards after a missed green. A chip-in counts too. This is not identical to scrambling, which asks whether par or better was saved after any missed green. Pitches from 30 to 50 yards are shown separately.

I take 32 putts per 18 holes. That total depends on how many greens I hit and where first putts start, as well as putting skill. My 8 three-putts followed first putts averaging 39 feet, against 19 feet on the holes I two-putted.

Three-putts
Three-putts. Three or more putts on one hole. What matters is what preceded them: the length of the first putt. The data records where each putt started but not how far the second one was left, so I can say long first putts come before three-putts, and not whether the lag or the short one was the miss.

Leaks
What a focus-area share means. A candidate area losing strokes in this sample. The percentage is its share of the three displayed focus estimates, not of all strokes lost. These estimates overlap with category totals and are not guaranteed recoverable strokes or a forecast of a lower handicap.
Too few to read
"Too few to read". A distance bucket with fewer than 8 shots behind it is greyed out and never chosen as a focus area. Three good chips in a row is not evidence. Under 3 shots it is not published at all.
How practice follows
How practice follows from this. The leak table is read straight into my practice sessions: the top leaks become the blocks I hit, and the session is built around them rather than around whatever I feel like practising. That is also why a bucket with too few shots is never allowed to rank, because a fluke would set the agenda for weeks.

What this data cannot tell me

This is built on 5 rounds and 81 holes. That is enough to be useful and not enough to be certain, and the honest version of this page says which is which.

A small and selected sample
Only sensor-recorded rounds can supply shot analysis. This sample may not represent my recent form or all the conditions I play in. An interval crossing zero means the result is inconclusive.
Some lies are inferred
Tee and putter positions are recognized. The first shot after a par-4/5 tee shot uses the fairway flag to infer fairway or rough; other non-putting lies are assumed fairway. Sand and recovery situations are not reliably distinguished, which can move strokes between categories.
Distances answer different questions
On-course driver distances include roll and omit penalty drives. TrackMan carry is distance to landing. Neither should be substituted for the other.
First-putt data is limited
Distance-band tables use first putts. Later-putt positions are not reliable enough to diagnose lag leaves, even though later strokes contribute to overall putting totals.
The scratch model was calibrated on this sample
Expected-strokes tables were assembled locally and tuned to match the Arccos category dashboard on the first five rounds. Matching those totals is not independent validation of each bucket, causal explanation or confidence interval.

When any of that changes, these pages change with it. They rebuild from the data every morning when the pipeline runs. Rebuilding does not make old rounds current; coaching and goals need review.
