Short game

Non-putting shots from 50 yards or closer. The aim is to finish the hole efficiently, not simply to hit a good-looking chip.

How to read these pages

What the numbers say

  1. Short game costs 4.2 strokes a round, the most of any part. The range, −6.2 to −2.1 a round, is all below zero, so in this sample the loss is real.
  2. Every distance band loses. 0–20 yd −1.9 (34 shots), 20–35 yd −1.2 (21 shots) and 35–50 yd −1.1 (12 shots), per round.
  3. Up and down: 9 of 31 inside 30 yd, 0 of 16 from 30 to 50. That is 29% inside 30 yd. Counting every missed green, I saved par 13 times in 54 (24%); the PGA Tour average is 59%.
  4. The short game swings the most from round to round. It lost 7.9 in the 91, lost 8.3 in the 87 and lost 0.6 in the 82, my lowest. No other part varied by more than 5.2 strokes across these rounds.

Up and down, Too few to read, Strokes gained

Up and down. Here: finish the hole in two strokes or fewer, including penalties, from the first non-putting shot inside 30 yards after a missed green. A chip-in counts too. This is not identical to scrambling, which asks whether par or better was saved after any missed green. Pitches from 30 to 50 yards are shown separately.

"Too few to read". A distance bucket with fewer than 8 shots behind it is greyed out and never chosen as a focus area. Three good chips in a row is not evidence. Under 3 shots it is not published at all.

Strokes gained. Expected strokes before the shot, minus expected strokes afterward, minus the shot and any penalties. Positive is better than the scratch model; negative is worse. −2.0 per 18 holes means about two strokes lost. Category totals include all shots; the putting distance table covers first putts only.

On-course analysis: 5 rounds / 81 holes, 2026-04-15 to 2026-06-04. 5 Arccos rounds, Apr 2026 to Jun 2026, 81 holes; strokes gained against a locally calibrated scratch baseline, not an independent Arccos calculation. Tee and green positions are identified; the first shot after a par-4/5 tee shot uses the fairway flag to infer fairway or rough. Other non-putting lies are assumed fairway.
