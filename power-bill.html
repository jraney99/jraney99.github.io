The $1.8M power bill

Three sets of numbers that should have matched, and another customer’s charges on our power bill.

MARA · Lead Financial Analyst, Operations & Energy Transformation · 2025 to 2026

$1.8M

in billing errors, found by lining up the meters, our reports and the invoices

Power is the biggest cost in bitcoin mining. At MARA I was the only analyst under the VP of Finance for 18 sites drawing 1.8 GW, with $516 million a year in power costs. Disbursements, power bills included, came to me for first approval before the VP and the CFO, and no payment was missed or duplicated.

A power bill is hard to argue with. The utility works out the total on its side, from meter reads, rates and charges, and what arrives is a number that looks a lot like last month’s. If one of the inputs is wrong, nothing on the page tells you.

The three-way match

I was asked to look into a question about our power bills. To answer it, I built a check no one had run before, putting three records side by side:

  1. Interval meter data, which records what each site actually drew
  2. Our internal reporting in DOMO
  3. The utility’s invoices

Where they disagreed, I traced the difference back to where it started. The extra charges belonged to an unaffiliated industrial customer and had been billed against MARA’s power supply. I documented it until the case held up: $1.8 million in cumulative billing errors, with proof of where they came from. I handed the case off when I left MARA in April 2026.

Then I rebuilt and wrote down the energy-audit procedures for all 18 sites, so every site’s invoices were reviewed and approved the same way.
