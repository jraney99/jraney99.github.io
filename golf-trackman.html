How to read these pages

Three records: GHIN supplies posted scores and the official Handicap Index; Arccos supplies sensor shots for a local scratch comparison; TrackMan supplies practice measurements.

Strokes gained (SG): +1 means one stroke better than the scratch model; −1 means one worse. Summary rates are per 18 holes unless labeled per shot or actual round. Scratch means a 0.0 handicap.

Median: the middle result. Carry: flight to landing. Total distance: includes roll. Dispersion: how widely shots scatter. Expand the explanations beside a chart for its exact definition.

Evidence: samples are labeled, small groups are flagged, and uncertainty ranges do not account for every model assumption. A daily rebuild does not mean the underlying rounds are recent.

Ball speed
Ball speed, club speed and smash factor. Smash factor is ball speed divided by clubhead speed. Around 1.50 is a useful driver reference, not a universal hard ceiling. Strike location, delivered loft and measurement conditions all matter; a low ratio alone does not prove an off-center strike.

The corridor
The practice corridor. A 36-yard-wide strip, 18 yards either side of the target line. This uses TrackMan lateral position at carry, not fairways hit on the course. It is a practice accuracy measure; it does not reproduce hazards, slopes or penalty outcomes.
Spread
How TrackMan spread is calculated. The spread is half the distance between the 10th and 90th percentiles. It describes the width of the middle 80% of recorded shots. It need not be symmetric around the median or target. The outer tails remain in the data, but this number does not describe them.

Inferred swing lengths
Why swing lengths are "inferred". TrackMan records the ball, not the backswing. The wedge matrix groups each club’s shots by carry and calls the groups short, stock and full, which is a guess from the result. It only appears for a club with enough shots and clear separation between them, and it is never presented as something measured.

How it was built

  • The driver was ordered as a head only. Ping does not sell the head on its own, so it came as a stock build.
  • The fitting order specifies the grip, ZGRIP Cord midsize, logo down. Ping does not offer that one in midsize, so their answer is a double wrap of the standard size. The alternative was standard Tour Velvet and a regrip by me.
  • Swingweight is C-9 for the Ping irons and wedges. The rep's note was that the irons and the 58 would come out a touch over, C9+, and that the difference between the two is about the weight of a dollar bill.
  • Black Dot is Ping's standard lie. The Ping irons and wedges are a quarter inch over standard length. The three-wood has standard shaft length and standard lie.

Swingweight
Swingweight. How heavy a club feels through the swing rather than what it weighs on a scale: the balance between head and grip, written as a letter and a number. C9 is slightly lighter than the D0 to D2 most stock clubs are built to. The gap between C9 and C9+ is about the weight of a dollar bill, which is why the wedges can sit either side of it without feeling different.
Lie angle
Lie angle. The angle between the shaft and the ground when the club is soled as designed. Delivered lie can influence face direction, particularly with more loft. A one-sided miss can also come from aim, face, path or contact, so dispersion alone cannot diagnose a lie adjustment.
