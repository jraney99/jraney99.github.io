Ball speed
Ball speed, club speed and smash factor. Club speed is how fast the head is moving, ball speed is how fast the ball leaves, and smash factor is one divided by the other. It is a strike-quality number: about 1.50 off a driver is the ceiling, and anything well under it means the ball is not being found in the middle.

How it is graded
How a session is graded. Against the plan it was given, not against a perfect session. Only two things are graded: whether the driver stayed in the corridor and whether the wedges carried consistent distances. Carry accuracy against a target is not graded at all, because the export records no intended target.

Gapping
Gapping. The carry distance between one club and the next. Even gaps mean every yardage has a club; a 25 yard hole between two of them is a shot that has to be hit soft or hard, which is where approach misses come from. A gap that is too small means a club is doing nothing.
Carry
Carry, and why it is not the number on the sign. Carry is where the ball lands, not where it finishes. On the course it rolls out, sometimes a long way, so a 150 yard carry is not a 150 yard club on firm ground. The gapping table below uses carry because it is the part a swing controls.

The corridor
The corridor. A 36 yard wide strip, 18 either side of the target line, which is roughly a generous fairway. The driver figure is the share of balls that finished inside it, which is the practice version of the penalty problem on the course.
Spread
Spread, and why it is not the average. The middle 80% of shots with a club, halved. A 12 yard spread means a typical shot lands within 12 yards either side of the usual one. It ignores the two or three worst strikes of a session on purpose, because one cold top says nothing about a gap.

Inferred swing lengths
Why swing lengths are "inferred". TrackMan records the ball, not the backswing. The wedge matrix groups each club’s shots by carry and calls the groups short, stock and full, which is a guess from the result. It only appears for a club with enough shots and clear separation between them, and it is never presented as something measured.

How it was built

  • The driver was ordered as a head only. Ping does not sell the head on its own, so it came as a stock build.
  • Every club has the same grip, ZGRIP Cord midsize, logo down. Ping does not offer that one in midsize, so their answer is a double wrap of the standard size. The alternative was standard Tour Velvet and a regrip by me.
  • Swingweight is C-9 across the set. The rep's note was that the irons and the 58 would come out a touch over, C9+, and that the difference between the two is about the weight of a dollar bill.
  • Black Dot is Ping's standard lie. Everything is a quarter inch over standard length.

Ball: Titleist Pro V1x. As ordered through the Oly fitting, October 2026. Build details still missing for the 3 wood, putter.

Swingweight
Swingweight. How heavy a club feels through the swing rather than what it weighs on a scale: the balance between head and grip, written as a letter and a number. C9 is slightly lighter than the D0 to D2 most stock clubs are built to. The gap between C9 and C9+ is about the weight of a dollar bill, which is why the wedges can sit either side of it without feeling different.
Lie angle
Lie angle, and why it shows up in the dispersion. The angle between the shaft and the ground at address. Too upright and the face points left at impact, too flat and it points right, by a few yards per degree at full-iron distance. It is the first thing worth checking against a one-sided miss on the dispersion table, before anything in the swing.
